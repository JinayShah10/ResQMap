==================================================

TITLE

First Aid for Poisoning

==================================================

OVERVIEW

Poisoning occurs when a harmful substance is swallowed, inhaled, absorbed through the skin, or splashed into the eyes. It is highly dangerous because toxins can damage internal organs and disrupt breathing or heart function. Immediate contact with poison control is vital.

==================================================

COMMON SIGNS

Nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain
Difficulty breathing or sudden coughing
Dizziness, confusion, or drowsiness
Chemical burns around the mouth or on the skin
Unusual odors on the breath or clothing

==================================================

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS

1. Remove the victim from the source of the poison immediately.
2. Call your local poison control center or emergency services.
3. Flush the skin or eyes with running water for fifteen minutes if exposed.
4. Save the container of the suspected poison for identification.

==================================================

DO NOT

Do not induce vomiting unless specifically instructed by poison control.
Do not give the victim anything to drink or eat unless advised by professionals.
Do not wait for symptoms to develop before calling for help.

==================================================

WHEN TO CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES

Call emergency services immediately if the victim is unresponsive or has difficulty breathing.
Call if the victim is having seizures or is highly agitated.
Call if the victim has swallowed a corrosive chemical or battery.

==================================================

UNTIL HELP ARRIVES

Keep the victim calm and comfortable.
Monitor their breathing and heart rate closely.
Be prepared to perform CPR if the victim stops breathing.
Follow the advice given by poison control specialists exactly.

==================================================

PREVENTION

Store household chemicals and medicines in locked cabinets.
Keep products in their original containers with warning labels intact.
Install carbon monoxide detectors in your home.
Dispose of expired medications and toxic substances safely.
