==================================================

TITLE

Emergency Action for Water Rescue

==================================================

OVERVIEW

Water rescue is required when a person is drowning or struggling in water. It is a critical emergency because asphyxiation from water inhalation leads to brain damage and cardiac arrest within minutes. Ensuring the rescuer's safety is the primary rule to prevent double drowning.

==================================================

COMMON SIGNS

Victim splashing, gasping for air, or bobbing up and down in water
Victim's head low in the water with mouth at water level
Inability to swim or call for help
Victim floating face down in the water

==================================================

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS

1. Shout for help and call emergency services immediately.
2. Use the reach-throw-row-go method: reach out with a long pole or stick.
3. Throw a flotation device, rope, or empty plastic jug to the victim.
4. Row a boat to the victim if available.
5. Swim to the victim only as a last resort and if you are a trained lifeguard.

==================================================

DO NOT

Do not jump into the water to rescue someone unless you are a trained lifesaver, as struggling victims can pull you down.
Do not let the victim grab your neck or body during a rescue.
Do not ignore a silent victim, as drowning is often silent.

==================================================

WHEN TO CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES

Call emergency services immediately if someone is struggling or submerging in water.
Call if a child is missing near a swimming pool or natural water body.
Call if the rescued victim is unresponsive or coughing up water.

==================================================

UNTIL HELP ARRIVES

Pull the victim out of the water as quickly and safely as possible.
Place the victim on their back on a firm surface.
Open the airway and check for breathing.
Start CPR immediately if they are not breathing, beginning with rescue breaths.

==================================================

PREVENTION

Learn to swim and float in water.
Wear life jackets when boating, fishing, or participating in water sports.
Never swim alone or under the influence of alcohol.
Fence all home swimming pools and supervise children near water.
