==================================================

TITLE

First Aid for Dog Bites

==================================================

OVERVIEW

Dog bites can cause deep puncture wounds, tissue tearing, and severe infections. The primary danger is the transmission of bacteria and the rabies virus, which is fatal if untreated. Immediate thorough cleaning and medical evaluation are necessary.

==================================================

COMMON SIGNS

Puncture wounds, cuts, or tears in the skin
Bleeding from the wound
Pain, swelling, or redness around the bite site
Warmth or pus draining from the wound

==================================================

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS

1. Wash the bite wound immediately with soap and warm running water.
2. Flush the wound thoroughly for at least five minutes.
3. Apply direct pressure with a clean cloth to control bleeding.
4. Apply an antibiotic ointment to the wound.
5. Cover the bite with a clean sterile bandage.

==================================================

DO NOT

Do not ignore a dog bite even if the skin is barely broken.
Do not apply tight bandages that trap bacteria inside the wound.
Do not try to handle or capture an aggressive animal.

==================================================

WHEN TO CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES

Call emergency services if the bleeding is severe and does not stop.
Call if the bite is on the face, neck, hands, or near joints.
Call if the dog is unknown, stray, or shows signs of rabies.
Call if the wound appears deep or shows signs of infection.

==================================================

UNTIL HELP ARRIVES

Keep the wounded area elevated if possible.
Monitor the victim for signs of fever or increasing redness.
Find out if the dog is up to date on its rabies vaccinations.
Clean the area gently if dressing needs changing.

==================================================

PREVENTION

Avoid approaching unfamiliar dogs or animals.
Do not disturb a dog that is eating, sleeping, or caring for puppies.
Ensure your own pets are fully vaccinated against rabies.
Teach children to remain still if approached by an off-leash dog.
