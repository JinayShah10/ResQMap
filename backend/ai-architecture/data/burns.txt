==================================================

TITLE

First Aid for Burns

==================================================

OVERVIEW

Burns are tissue damage resulting from heat, chemicals, electricity, radiation, or friction. Severe burns can lead to critical fluid loss, systemic infection, and life-threatening shock. Immediate intervention is required to stop the burning process, cool the affected area, and protect the skin barrier.

==================================================

COMMON SIGNS

Red or discolored skin
Blisters forming on the surface
Severe pain or numbness in the area
Peeling or charred black skin
Swelling around the burn site

==================================================

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS

1. Remove the victim from the heat source immediately.
2. Cool the burn using cool running water for at least ten minutes.
3. Remove jewelry or tight clothing near the burn before swelling begins.
4. Cover the burn loosely with a clean sterile non-stick dressing.

==================================================

DO NOT

Do not apply ice because extreme cold can cause further tissue damage.
Do not pop blisters since open blisters increase the risk of infection.
Do not apply butter or grease because they trap heat inside the burn.
Do not remove clothing stuck to the burn as this rips the skin.

==================================================

WHEN TO CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES

Call emergency services immediately if the burn covers a large area.
Call if the burn is on the face, hands, feet, groin, or major joints.
Call if the burn is chemical or electrical.
Call if the skin appears charred, white, or leathery.

==================================================

UNTIL HELP ARRIVES

Monitor the victim for signs of shock or breathing difficulty.
Keep the victim warm using a clean blanket.
Keep the burned limb elevated above heart level if possible.
Reassure the victim to keep them calm.

==================================================

PREVENTION

Keep hot liquids and foods away from table edges.
Install smoke alarms on every level of the home.
Store chemical agents in locked cabinets.
Set water heater thermostats below one hundred and twenty degrees Fahrenheit.
