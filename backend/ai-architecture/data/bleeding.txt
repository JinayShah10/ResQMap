==================================================

TITLE

Control of Severe Bleeding

==================================================

OVERVIEW

Severe bleeding is the rapid loss of blood from damaged blood vessels. Uncontrolled bleeding can lead to cardiovascular shock, organ failure, and death within minutes. Immediate direct pressure is critical to stop blood loss and promote clotting.

==================================================

COMMON SIGNS

Blood spurting or flowing continuously from a wound
Pooling of blood on the ground or clothing
Wounds with deep tissue exposure
Victim feeling dizzy, cold, or pale

==================================================

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS

1. Apply direct pressure to the wound using a clean cloth.
2. Maintain constant firm pressure without releasing it.
3. Elevate the bleeding limb above the level of the heart.
4. Apply a sterile bandage tightly over the dressing.
5. Apply a tourniquet high on the limb if bleeding does not stop.

==================================================

DO NOT

Do not remove the original dressing if blood seeps through, add more layers instead.
Do not remove embedded objects because they act as a plug.
Do not wash deep or severely bleeding wounds as this disrupts clotting.

==================================================

WHEN TO CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES

Call emergency services immediately if bleeding is severe and does not stop.
Call if blood is spurting from the wound.
Call if the victim shows signs of shock such as paleness or confusion.
Call if the wound is deep and requires stitches.

==================================================

UNTIL HELP ARRIVES

Keep the victim lying down and quiet.
Cover the victim with a blanket to prevent hypothermia.
Monitor the victim's breathing and consciousness levels continuously.
Ensure the bandage remains tight and dry.

==================================================

PREVENTION

Store sharp instruments in protective sheaths.
Wear appropriate personal protective gear when handling machinery.
Keep first aid kits fully stocked with bandages and tourniquets.
