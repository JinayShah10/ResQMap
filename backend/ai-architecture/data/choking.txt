==================================================

TITLE

First Aid for Choking

==================================================

OVERVIEW

Choking occurs when a foreign object blocks the airway, preventing normal breathing. It is a critical emergency because oxygen deprivation can lead to unconsciousness and brain damage in minutes. Immediate abdominal thrusts are required to dislodge the obstruction.

==================================================

COMMON SIGNS

Inability to speak or cry out
Clutching the throat with hands
Difficulty breathing or high-pitched squeaking sounds
Bluish skin, lips, or nails
Weak or ineffective coughing

==================================================

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS

1. Ask the person if they are choking.
2. Stand behind the person and lean them slightly forward.
3. Wrap your arms around the person's waist.
4. Make a fist with one hand.
5. Place the thumb side of your fist just above the navel.
6. Grasp your fist with your other hand.
7. Press into the abdomen with quick upward thrusts.

==================================================

DO NOT

Do not perform abdominal thrusts on a pregnant woman or an infant.
Do not perform a blind finger sweep because you might push the object deeper.
Do not give the person water or food as it blocks the airway further.

==================================================

WHEN TO CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES

Call emergency services immediately if the choking victim becomes unconscious.
Call if the airway remains completely blocked after several attempts to clear it.
Call if the victim has difficulty breathing after the object is removed.

==================================================

UNTIL HELP ARRIVES

Lower the unconscious victim gently to the floor.
Begin chest compressions immediately if they become unresponsive.
Look inside the mouth during compressions and remove visible objects.
Ensure the head remains tilted back to keep the airway open.

==================================================

PREVENTION

Cut food into small bite-sized pieces before eating.
Chew food thoroughly before swallowing.
Keep small items away from infants and young children.
Avoid talking or laughing while chewing.
