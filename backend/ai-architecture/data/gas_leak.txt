==================================================

TITLE

Emergency Action for Gas Leaks

==================================================

OVERVIEW

Gas leaks involve the release of flammable gases like natural gas or propane, which can cause sudden explosions, fire, and asphyxiation. The primary danger is that a tiny spark can ignite the gas, causing a catastrophic blast. Immediate evacuation and ventilation are critical.

==================================================

COMMON SIGNS

Smell of sulfur or rotten eggs
Hissing or whistling sound near gas appliances or pipes
Dead or dying plants near a gas line
Dizziness, nausea, or headache in a confined space

==================================================

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS

1. Evacuate the area immediately and move to a safe outdoor location.
2. Open windows and doors if you can do so quickly.
3. Do not turn on or off any electrical switches as they create sparks.
4. Do not use matches, lighters, or phones inside the building.
5. Shut off the main gas supply valve if it is located outside and accessible.

==================================================

DO NOT

Do not use landlines or cell phones inside the building with the leak.
Do not start motor vehicles near the suspected gas leak.
Do not turn on light switches, appliances, or flashlights as they create small sparks.

==================================================

WHEN TO CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES

Call emergency services from a safe distance outside the building.
Call the gas utility company immediately from a safe phone location.
Call if anyone is experiencing dizziness, nausea, or breathing difficulties.

==================================================

UNTIL HELP ARRIVES

Wait in a safe outdoor location far away from the building.
Ensure no one attempts to re-enter the building until emergency services declare it safe.
Monitor any affected persons for breathing issues.
Keep onlookers away from the area.

==================================================

PREVENTION

Schedule regular professional inspections of gas appliances and piping.
Install combustible gas detectors near fuel-burning appliances.
Know the location of and how to operate the main gas shutoff valve.
Never store flammable materials near gas water heaters or furnaces.

KEYWORDS
