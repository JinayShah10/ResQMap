TITLE

First Aid for Heart Attacks

==================================================

OVERVIEW

A heart attack occurs when blood flow to a part of the heart muscle is blocked, usually by a clot. This lack of oxygen causes tissue damage and can lead to fatal heart rhythms. Immediate administration of aspirin and calling emergency services are critical to save lives.

==================================================

COMMON SIGNS

Chest pain, pressure, squeezing, or fullness in the center of the chest
Pain radiating to the jaw, neck, back, or arms
Shortness of breath with or without chest discomfort
Cold sweat, lightheadedness, or sudden nausea
Unexplained fatigue or weakness

==================================================

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS

1. Have the victim sit down and rest immediately.
2. Call emergency services and request an automated external defibrillator.
3. Loosen tight clothing around the neck and chest.
4. Ask the victim if they have prescribed nitroglycerin and assist them in taking it.
5. Administer one adult aspirin if the victim is not allergic.
6. Have the victim chew the aspirin slowly.

==================================================

DO NOT

Do not leave the victim alone unless absolutely necessary to call for help.
Do not let the victim walk or exert themselves under any circumstances.
Do not give the victim water or food as they may choke.
Do not ignore symptoms thinking they are simple indigestion.

==================================================

WHEN TO CALL EMERGENCY SERVICES

Call emergency services immediately if any signs of a heart attack appear.
Call if chest pain lasts more than a few minutes or goes away and returns.
Call if the victim collapses and becomes unresponsive.

==================================================

UNTIL HELP ARRIVES

Keep the victim calm and sitting upright.
Monitor their breathing and responsiveness continuously.
Locate the nearest automated external defibrillator.
Prepare to start CPR immediately if the victim stops breathing.

==================================================

PREVENTION

Maintain a healthy diet low in saturated fats and cholesterol.
Exercise regularly as recommended by your physician.
Avoid smoking and limit alcohol consumption.
Monitor and control blood pressure and blood sugar levels.
